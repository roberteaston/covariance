## Lie algebras
---

> [!note] Definition of Lie algebra
> Let $k$ be a field. A **Lie algebra** over $k$ is a $k$-vector space, $V$, equipped with a bilinear, skew-symmetric map (usually called the **Lie bracket**)
> $$[-,-]:V\times V \to V$$
> that satisfies the *Jacobi identity*:
> $$[x,[y,z]]+[z,[x,y]]+[y,[x,x]]=0$$
> for all $x,y,z\in V$.

> [!warning] Conflicting definitions
> Here I'm using the definition of Lie algebra as presented in nLab, which is generally a reliable reference. In Wikipedia, however, the skew-commutativity is replaced with an "alternating property", namely that $[x,x]=0$ for all $x\in $V$. It is then noted that skew-commutativity implies the alternating property over fields of characteristic not $2$.
> 
> A more intrinsic definition of Lie algebras would settle this confusion ...


> [!note] Note on notation
> For historical reasons, it is common to use Gothic letters (e.g., $\mathfrak{g}$, $\mathfrak{h}$, $\mathfrak{V}$, etc.) to denote Lie algebras, especially Lie algebras of given Lie groups.
